Unified Mark Scheme — Task 1 Activity A (18 marks)

This single-page mark scheme displays the generic banding criteria alongside the specific, contextualised requirements for Assignments A, B, and C. The model answers in the other tabs are designed to meet the Band 4 criteria across all columns.

4 15–18 Comprehensive risk register covering a wide range of risks. Each risk includes threat, likelihood, vulnerability, asset, impact, control type(s), risk response, and risk process stage, all accurately applied. Legislation (GDPR/DPA 2018) and NCSC Cyber Essentials are cited appropriately and accurately. Focuses on full security register. Explicitly maps risks to control types, risk responses, process stages, and cites GDPR/DPA 2018 & NCSC Cyber Essentials. Focuses on technical and environmental threats (SPOF, ISP, capacity, power). Includes control types, risk responses, and process stages. Focuses on DRP/BCP risks. Includes RTO/RPO/MTBF/MTTR calculations, encryption at rest/in transit, and risk responses.
3 11–14 Detailed risk register covering a range of risks. Most risks include all required columns, with control types and risk responses mostly correct. Legislation and NCSC guidance cited, with minor inaccuracies. Minor inaccuracies in legislation or missing control types for some risks. Minor inaccuracies in risk responses or missing process stages for some risks. Minor inaccuracies in RTO/RPO calculations or missing encryption details.
2 6–10 Adequate risk register covering some risks. Some required columns missing or partially completed. Control types and risk responses applied inconsistently. Legislation or NCSC guidance referenced superficially. Security risks listed but lacks explicit control types or legislation references. Infrastructure risks listed but lacks explicit risk responses or process stages. Continuity risks listed but lacks RTO/RPO calculations or encryption details.
1 1–5 Limited risk register. Few risks identified. Required columns largely missing. Little or no reference to legislation or NCSC guidance. Minimal security detail. Minimal infrastructure detail. Minimal continuity detail.
0 0 No creditable response.
How the model answers achieve Band 4:
  • Wide range of risks: 6–7 highly contextualised threats per assignment.
  • All 8 columns applied: The official Pearson template only has 6 columns. The model answers use the 'Additional information' column to explicitly state the Control Type, Risk Response, and Risk Process Stage to hit the Band 4 requirement.
  • Justified Likelihood: As seen in the official Pearson exemplar, the likelihood column includes a reason for the rating, proving the student has contextualised the risk.
  • Legislation/NCSC: Explicitly cited in Assignment A and Assignment C, and mapped to best practice in Assignment B.
Assignment A (Northgate Health Trust): Security-focused risk register. Includes contextualised threats for a healthcare satellite clinic (IoT devices, supply chain). The 'Additional information' column explicitly names Control Types, Risk Responses, Process Stages, and cites GDPR/DPA 2018 & NCSC Cyber Essentials.

Phishing email / social engineering targeting clinical staff to steal domain credentials. High Healthcare staff are frequently targeted by sophisticated phishing campaigns. The clinic handles a high volume of external emails, increasing exposure. Staff lack phishing awareness. No Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) enabled on domain accounts. Patient-adjacent records (names, addresses, appointment data) on the fileserver. Data breach resulting in UK GDPR Article 5 & 32 non-compliance. ICO fine up to £17.5m. Reputational damage.

Control Type: Preventative (Email filtering, MFA), Detective (SIEM alerts).

Risk Response: Mitigate (reduce likelihood via training).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (High) → Impact (High) → Prioritise (Critical) → Mitigate.

Legislation: GDPR Art 32; NCSC Cyber Essentials (User Access Control).
Ransomware delivered via malicious attachment or drive-by download. High Ransomware is the most common cyber threat to UK healthcare. The lack of EDR and local admin rights makes successful execution highly probable. Unpatched endpoint OS. Users have local admin rights. Backup drive is permanently connected. Fileserver shared folders (company, clinical admin, finance). Loss of availability of critical data. Clinic operations halted. Potential data exfiltration.

Control Type: Preventative (EDR, patching, least privilege), Corrective (offline backup).

Risk Response: Mitigate (layered controls), Transfer (cyber insurance).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (High) → Impact (Critical) → Prioritise (Critical) → Mitigate/Transfer.

Legislation: GDPR Art 32; NCSC Cyber Essentials (Malware Protection).
Insider threat: Unauthorised access by a finance user to clinical admin records. Medium Occurs occasionally due to human curiosity or error. The lack of RBAC means a user could accidentally stumble across files they shouldn't see. Flat file share permissions. No Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) implemented. No file auditing. Clinical admin shared folder. Unauthorised access to patient data. Breach of confidentiality. Disciplinary action required.

Control Type: Preventative (RBAC, least privilege), Directive (Acceptable Use Policy), Detective (File access auditing).

Risk Response: Mitigate (RBAC reduces likelihood and impact).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (Medium) → Impact (High) → Prioritise (High) → Mitigate.

Legislation: GDPR Art 5 (Integrity & Confidentiality); DPA 2018.
Wireless network compromise (attacker on another floor of shared building). Medium The building is shared with other tenants. While not actively targeted, opportunistic war-driving or rogue devices could attempt to connect. WPA2 with weak PSK. No WPA3 support. No RADIUS authentication. Guest network not isolated. Wireless network; domain credentials; patient-adjacent data accessible over Wi-Fi. Unauthorised network access. Interception of unencrypted data in transit.

Control Type: Preventative (WPA2-Enterprise/WPA3, RADIUS), Compensating (VLAN isolation).

Risk Response: Mitigate (upgrade to WPA3).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (Medium) → Impact (High) → Prioritise (High) → Mitigate.

Legislation: GDPR Art 32; NCSC Cyber Essentials (Secure Configuration).
Medical Device / IoT Compromise (e.g., networked printer or diagnostic machine). Medium Medical devices often run unpatched firmware and use default passwords. The likelihood increases as more devices are connected to the network. Default credentials on devices. Unpatched firmware. Devices placed on the same VLAN as staff PCs. Clinical VLAN network segment; patient data on the fileserver. Attacker uses the device as a pivot point to launch attacks against the fileserver or intercept traffic.

Control Type: Preventative (Change default passwords, patch firmware, network segmentation).

Risk Response: Mitigate (isolate IoT devices on a dedicated VLAN).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (Medium) → Impact (High) → Prioritise (High) → Mitigate.

Legislation: GDPR Art 32; NCSC Cyber Essentials (Secure Configuration).
Accidental Data Leakage by Staff (GDPR incident). High Human error is the leading cause of data breaches. With unrestricted USB ports and no DLP, the chance of accidental leakage is significant. No Data Loss Prevention (DLP). USB ports are unrestricted. Staff are not trained on data handling. Patient-adjacent records; emails; printed documents. ICO investigation, heavy fines (up to £17.5m), mandatory breach reporting, severe reputational damage.

Control Type: Directive (Data Handling Policy), Preventative (USB restriction GPO, DLP).

Risk Response: Mitigate (training and technical controls).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (High) → Impact (Critical) → Prioritise (Critical) → Mitigate.

Legislation: GDPR Art 5 & 32; DPA 2018.
Assignment B (Meridian Logistics): Infrastructure-focused risk register. Narrower focus on technical and environmental threats (SPOF, power, capacity). Added threats for physical cable damage and network loops to secure top-band marks.

Threat that could cause harm Likelihood of the threat occurring Vulnerabilities, or weaknesses in the security Asset that needs to be protected Possible impact, or damage if the threat occurs Additional information
Single point of failure: Core switch hardware failure. Medium Hardware failure is a known event over a 3–5 year lifecycle. Without dual PSU or a redundant switch, a single failure halts the network. No redundant switch. No dual power supply. No UPS for the switch. Core switch; all VLAN traffic (Operations and Finance). Total network outage for both departments. Loss of availability; operations halted.

Control Type: Preventative (Redundant switch, dual PSU, UPS), Corrective (Failover).

Risk Response: Mitigate (redundancy reduces likelihood and impact).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (Medium) → Impact (High) → Prioritise (High) → Mitigate.
Network dropout: ISP link failure. Medium ISPs experience occasional outages due to maintenance or cable damage. A single link with no failover increases the likelihood of a prolonged outage. Single ISP connection. No 4G/5G failover or secondary link. Internet connectivity; remote access; cloud services. Loss of internet access. Remote workers disconnected. Operations disrupted.

Control Type: Preventative (Secondary ISP, 4G failover), Detective (Link monitoring).

Risk Response: Mitigate (secondary link reduces likelihood).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (Medium) → Impact (High) → Prioritise (High) → Mitigate.
Server capacity exhaustion. Medium Data growth and application updates naturally increase resource consumption. Without monitoring, it is likely to become an issue within 12–18 months. No performance monitoring. No capacity thresholds or scaling plan. Server (AD DS, DNS, DHCP, file server). Slow performance. Service unavailability. User productivity loss.

Control Type: Detective (Monitoring/alerts), Preventative (Capacity planning).

Risk Response: Mitigate (monitoring reduces impact).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (Medium) → Impact (Medium) → Prioritise (Medium) → Mitigate.
Power failure or cooling failure (HVAC outage). Low The building has a reliable mains supply and backup generator. However, HVAC failures can occur during extreme heatwaves. No UPS. No generator. No temperature monitoring in server room. Server, switch, WAP, backup drive. Unexpected shutdown due to overheating. Hardware damage. Data corruption.

Control Type: Preventative (UPS, cooling, temp sensors), Corrective (Graceful shutdown).

Risk Response: Mitigate (UPS/sensors reduce likelihood and impact).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (Low) → Impact (High) → Prioritise (Medium) → Mitigate.
Physical cable damage or Electromagnetic Interference (EMI). Medium The logistics office has heavy machinery and high foot traffic. Cables run near walkways, creating a genuine risk of accidental damage. Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP) cables run near heavy machinery. Cables exposed in walkways. Network connectivity; data integrity. Packet loss, network slowdowns, or total loss of connectivity for affected segments.

Control Type: Preventative (Use Shielded cables (STP), cable trunking, physical routing).

Risk Response: Mitigate (physical protection).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (Medium) → Impact (Medium) → Prioritise (Medium) → Mitigate.
Switching Loop / Broadcast Storm. High Users frequently plug unmanaged switches or devices into wall ports to expand connectivity. Without STP enabled, a loop is highly likely. No Spanning Tree Protocol (STP) enabled. No BPDU Guard. Users plugging in unmanaged switches. Entire switching infrastructure (VLAN 10 and 20). Network is flooded with broadcast traffic, causing a complete network outage within minutes.

Control Type: Preventative (Enable STP, BPDU Guard, PortFast, Port Security).

Risk Response: Mitigate (switch configuration hardening).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (High) → Impact (High) → Prioritise (High) → Mitigate.

Threat that could cause harm Likelihood of the threat occurring Vulnerabilities, or weaknesses in the security Asset that needs to be protected Possible impact, or damage if the threat occurs Additional information
Disk failure on the fileserver (the event that has already occurred). High A disk failure has already occurred recently. Without RAID or SMART monitoring, the likelihood of recurrence remains high. No RAID configured. No SMART monitoring. No tested backup. No off-site copy. Shared folders (company, production, finance); fileserver. Permanent data loss. Production halted. Finance records unavailable. Recovery cost.

Control Type: Preventative (RAID 1/5), Corrective (Scheduled backup), Compensating (Off-site).

Risk Response: Mitigate (RAID/Backup), Transfer (Cloud backup).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (High) → Impact (Critical) → Prioritise (Critical) → Mitigate.

DRP Data: RTO: 4 hours. RPO: 1 hour. MTTR: 4 hours.

Encryption: BitLocker at rest.
Ransomware encrypting the fileserver and connected backup drives. High The backup drive is permanently connected and there is no EDR. If ransomware enters the network, it will likely encrypt both the live data and the backup. No EDR. Backup drive always connected (vulnerable to encryption). No MFA. Fileserver; production media; finance records; backup drive. Loss of availability and integrity. Ransom cost. GDPR personal data breach.

Control Type: Preventative (EDR, MFA), Corrective (Offline/immutable backup).

Risk Response: Mitigate (layered controls), Transfer (Cyber insurance).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (High) → Impact (Critical) → Prioritise (Critical) → Mitigate.

DRP Data: RTO: 8 hours. RPO: 1 hour. MTTR: 8 hours.
Fire or flood in the server room. Low The area is not in a high-risk flood zone, and the building has modern fire detection. However, the impact would be catastrophic. No off-site backup. No fire suppression. No flood detection. Fileserver, switch, WAP, backup drive. Total loss of equipment and data. Business cannot operate.

Control Type: Preventative (Fire suppression), Corrective (Off-site/Cloud backup).

Risk Response: Mitigate (off-site backup), Transfer (Insurance).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (Low) → Impact (Critical) → Prioritise (High) → Mitigate.

DRP Data: RTO: 24 hours. RPO: 1 hour. MTTR: 24 hours.

Encryption: TLS in transit for replication.
Accidental deletion of production files by staff. High Human error is common in busy media environments. Without file versioning or recycle bin retention, a simple mistake can destroy hours of work. No file versioning. No recycle bin retention. No individual file backup. Production shared folder; individual media files. Loss of work. Production delay. Rework cost.

Control Type: Preventative (Training), Corrective (Versioning, Backup restore).

Risk Response: Mitigate (versioning reduces impact).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (High) → Impact (Medium) → Prioritise (High) → Mitigate.

DRP Data: RTO: 1 hour. RPO: 1 hour. MTTR: 1 hour.
Malicious plugin or cracked software installed by a production user. High Production staff often download plugins and tools from unverified sources. With local admin rights, there is a high chance of introducing malware. Users have local admin rights. No application whitelisting. Unrestricted internet access. Production workstations; fileserver; network. Silent installation of malware/ransomware, leading to data encryption and data breach.

Control Type: Preventative (Application Whitelisting, Least Privilege, EDR).

Risk Response: Mitigate (remove admin rights, enforce whitelisting).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (High) → Impact (Critical) → Prioritise (Critical) → Mitigate.

Legislation: GDPR Art 32.
Cloud Sync Failure (Versioning attack). Medium If cloud sync is configured to mirror local changes automatically, a ransomware encryption event will instantly overwrite the cloud backups. Cloud sync automatically overwrites files. No offline read-only copies. No version history enabled on the cloud platform. Cloud backups; production media. Ransomware encrypts local files, cloud sync uploads the encrypted files, destroying the good backups.

Control Type: Preventative (Enable cloud version history, soft delete).

Risk Response: Mitigate (configure cloud retention policies).

Process Stage: Identify → Probability (Medium) → Impact (Critical) → Prioritise (High) → Mitigate.

DRP Data: RTO: 4 hours. RPO: 24 hours.
